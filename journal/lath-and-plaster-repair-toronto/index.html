    Lath and plaster in older Toronto homes: repair, cover or replace?

    Patch, skim, overboard with drywall or strip to the studs: how to decide what to do with the plaster walls and ceilings in a pre-war Toronto house.

    By Ricarti Drywall · 24 November 2026

    IN SHORTSmall cracks and holes in sound plaster can be patched. Sagging or widely cracked ceilings are usually overboarded with drywall or stripped and replaced. Strip to the studs when the walls are open for wiring, plumbing or insulation anyway. Test for asbestos before disturbing old plaster or textured coatings.

    Much of Toronto's housing was built with plaster over wood lath: thin strips nailed across the studs, with plaster pushed through the gaps to grip. Done well, it lasts a century. When it starts to fail, homeowners face a choice between keeping it and replacing it, and the right answer depends on what is failing and what else is happening in the house.

    Repair: when the plaster is still sound

    Press gently on the wall or ceiling around a crack. If it feels solid, the plaster is still keyed to the lath and can be repaired: cracks taped and filled, holes patched with plaster or drywall inserts, and the area skimmed to blend. A former client wrote that we installed new drywall and also repaired a lath and plaster wall in the same house; the two can live side by side.

    Cover: overboarding

    If a ceiling sags or is cracked across its width, the plaster keys have probably broken. Rather than tearing it down, the ceiling can be screwed back to the joists and covered with a layer of drywall. This is less messy than demolition and keeps the old plaster as extra mass for sound. The trade-off is a small change in depth at trim, crown moulding and electrical boxes.

    Replace: strip to the studs

    Removing plaster makes sense when the walls must be opened anyway: new wiring, new plumbing, or insulating exterior walls that were never insulated. Once the studs are bare, insulation, a vapour barrier and new drywall bring the wall up to modern standards in one go.

    Open the wall only when it needs to be open. Then do everything inside it at once.

    Test before you disturb

    Some older plasters, joint compounds and textured ceiling coatings contain asbestos, and older paint layers may contain lead. Before any demolition or heavy sanding in an older home, have samples tested by a qualified lab. If they come back positive, removal must be handled under Ontario's asbestos regulations by people trained to do it.

    Keeping the character

    Plaster walls have a slightly softer, less perfect plane than new drywall, and that is part of an older home's character. When we patch or overboard next to original plaster, we feather the finish so the transition does not show. Crown mouldings and ceiling medallions can be protected and kept, as on a recent ceiling on Lauther Ave in Toronto.
