    What your drywall sub needs to price a townhouse block accurately

    Drawings, finish spec, fire separations, ceiling heights and board-ready dates: the information that turns a drywall bid from a guess into a number you can hold.

    By Ricarti Drywall · 24 November 2026

    IN SHORTSend architectural drawings with sections, the finish level by area, fire-rated assemblies between units, ceiling heights and bulkheads, the board-ready date and phasing, and site conditions such as access, heat and hoisting. Missing any of these is where change orders come from.

    Most drywall disputes on new builds start at the bid. A number priced from a floor plan and a guess will be wrong somewhere, and the difference shows up later as an extra. Here is what we ask builders and site supers for, and why.

    Drawings with sections

    Floor plans give us wall lengths. Sections and elevations give us ceiling heights, sloped ceilings, stair walls and double-height spaces, which change board size, labour and scaffolding. A full architectural set is best.

    Finish level by area

    Level 4 throughout, or Level 5 in great rooms, foyers and stair halls? Garage and mechanical rooms to fire tape only? Name it per area and every bidder is pricing the same job.

    Fire separations

    Townhouses and semis need rated separations between units, and garages need separation from the house. The rated assemblies on the drawings, board type, layers and framing, drive material cost and inspection steps. Flag them early.

    Bulkheads and details

    Bulkheads for ductwork, coffered ceilings, arches, niches and curved walls take time. If they are not on the drawings yet, tell us where you expect them.

    Schedule and phasing

    Board-ready dates for each unit or phase let us staff properly. Ask us what crew size we can commit, and hold us to it.

    Site conditions

    • Is temporary heat on? Compound needs above-freezing temperatures to cure properly in winter.
    • Can board be hoisted or delivered through openings, or does it go up the stairs?
    • Where can materials be stored, and where does waste go?
    The more you send, the fewer extras you sign.

    What you get back

    A per-unit or per-block price that names the board, insulation and finish levels, the exclusions, and a crew schedule. WSIB clearance and a certificate of insurance come with the quote, not on day one.
